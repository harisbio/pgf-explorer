How PGF-Explorer was made, and how to cite it

About

PGF-Explorer is a database of studies that identify a gene family across a whole plant genome. It records, for each study, what was studied (gene family, species, number of genes), how it was studied (search methods, tools, phylogeny, duplication analysis) and with what evidence (expression data, RT-qPCR and functional experiments).

The database grew out of a systematic review and meta-analysis of genome-wide gene family identification in plants. It makes the underlying records searchable and extends them to the wider literature, so that researchers can see what has already been done for a family or species before starting a new study.

Each extracted value is stored with the sentence it was taken from, and each paper links to its article page.

Scope

A study is included when all of the following hold:

  • it is an original research article, not a review, editorial or methods-only paper;
  • it identifies the members of a gene family (or superfamily) systematically from a sequenced genome or proteome, rather than cloning a few genes or using a transcriptome alone;
  • the study organism is a land plant (studies of algae alone are excluded);
  • it describes an in-silico identification workflow;
  • it is published in English between 2000 and 2025.

Studies of non-coding RNA families, pan-genome presence and absence studies, and studies in which plants are a minor part of a mainly non-plant analysis are excluded.

Sources

PubMed, Europe PMC and OpenAlex were searched for articles published from 2000 to 2025, using genome-wide and gene family terms together with a list of plant terms. Of 59,838 records identified, 15,998 titles and abstracts were screened after removing duplicates and clearly ineligible records. The eligible studies, together with the 310 studies of the meta-analysis, form the 9,993 records of the database.

Titles, authors, journals and DOIs were checked against Crossref. Corresponding-author countries, citation counts and field-weighted citation impact (FWCI) come from OpenAlex; citation data were retrieved in October 2026.

Extraction

Records were extracted with a purpose-built extraction program. It converts each article into section-labelled text; Claude Sonnet (Anthropic), run through Claude Code, fills the fields defined in a written codebook; and a validation module saves a record only when every value has its allowed format and is supported by a quote found in the article text.

After extraction, controlled vocabularies were applied: species names were standardized to binomials, gene family names and synonyms to one spelling (subfamilies are kept where the authors name them), tool names to a standard list, and duplication types to a fixed set of categories.

The extraction program, its codebook and validation module are openly available (GitHub, Zenodo).

How missing information is shown

  • Yes: the paper reports it.
  • — or Not reported: the extracted text does not report it; the study may still have done it.
  • Fields with no value are left out of a record's detail view.

A full description of every column is in the data dictionary.

Quality checks

  • Screening sensitivity. Papers already known to be eligible were mixed, unlabelled, into the screening; 97.9% of them were correctly included.
  • Extraction agreement. In a pilot of 60 papers, automated extraction agreed with values recorded by hand from the same papers for 95% of gene families and 88% of species.
  • Source checking. Every saved value had to be supported by a quote found in the article text; the quotes are published with the data.
  • Eligibility. Papers judged not to be genome-wide gene family studies on closer reading (for example, genes cloned individually, or genes taken from a database without a search) were removed.

Limitations

  • Papers do not always report every method in the text that was extracted, so a method shown as "—" may still have been used, and method shares are lower limits.
  • The search was limited to three databases and to English-language articles; some eligible studies, especially in regional journals, may be missing.
  • Quote checking confirms that each value is supported by text in the article, not that every interpretation is correct. Users should consult the article before relying on a single value.
  • Country refers to the corresponding author only.
  • Citation counts change over time; recent papers have had little time to be cited.

How to cite

The database

Haris M, Azeem F, Golokhvast KS, Nawaz MA. PGF-Explorer: a database of genome-wide gene family identification studies in plants. Version 1.0. 2026. https://harisbio.github.io/pgf-explorer/

The meta-analysis

Haris M, Azeem F, Golokhvast KS, Nawaz MA. Twenty-five years of genome-wide gene family identification analysis in plants: trends, tools, and methodological shifts. A systematic review and meta-analysis. Citation details will be added on publication.

Individual papers

When you use a record, please also cite the original article. Citations for every paper are on the Downloads page.
